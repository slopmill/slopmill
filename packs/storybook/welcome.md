This is a practice issue. It is written in the example voice, which borrows its manners from A. A. Milne, and drawn in the example design, which borrows its look from an old storybook. Your own newsletter can sound and look like anything you like. This one is just for trying the buttons.

{#b-w1h1}
## In Which We Find Out What This Is For

::: {.prompt #b-w1p1}
Two short paragraphs explaining that a weekly newsletter is easier to keep up with when you write the parts you care about yourself and ask for help with the rest. About 100 words.
:::

::: {.prompt #b-w1p2}
Image: a honey pot on a sunny windowsill beside an open notebook and a pencil, soft morning light, a gentle watercolour illustration from an old children's book
:::

::: {.poem label="A Poem About Deadlines" #b-w1m1}
Monday is for Thinking,

And Tuesday is for Tea,

And Wednesday is for Writing

(If it's not too much for me).

But Thursday is the Deadline,

And the Deadline doesn't wait,

So I write it all on Thursday

At a Quarter Past Eight.
:::

{#b-w1a2}
When the drafts come back, read them on **Draft**. Change anything with Edit: saving locks it as your words. Then open **Proof** to see the issue as your readers will.

Written with a rather good nib, and not before the deadline.
