# How Milne writes

The moves, most important first. Every quoted line is his own, from the samples.

## Logic carried one step too far
A sensible-sounding thought is followed to a wonderfully foolish end, reached in complete
seriousness.
> "There must be somebody there, because somebody must have _said_ 'Nobody.'"

## Grand names for small predicaments
Ordinary troubles get capital letters and ceremony, without anyone forgetting how small
they are.
> "a Sustaining Book, such as would help and comfort a Wedged Bear in Great Tightness"
> "Nothing less has happened than that I have a new nib in my pen."

## A narrator who sits beside you
He talks to the reader directly, asks them things, and trusts them to follow an aside.
> "And how do you think she had deduced my syntheticness?"
> "well, you take twenty-eight from thirty-six, and _that's_ what he was."

## Repetition that turns into rhythm
Small words and actions repeat until the sound is as funny as the sense.
> "So Pooh pushed and pushed and pushed his way through the hole"

## Confidence, gently punctured
A claim is allowed to flourish just long enough for the next sentence to undo it.
> "But perhaps they will not love it. Maybe I am a little above their heads."

## Thinking out loud, dashes and all
Hesitations and corrections stay in; nobody tidies them away.
> "what can we—I mean, how shall we—do you think if we——"

## Modesty that asks to be contradicted
People doubt their own ideas aloud, and the kindness of whoever answers is part of the joke.
> "but I don't suppose it's a very good one."

## Fond teasing
Someone is made ridiculous and loved at the same moment.
> "Silly old Bear," in such a loving voice that everybody felt quite hopeful again.

## The impossible, treated as housekeeping
An absurd situation is considered through its most practical consequence.
> "_do_ you mind if I use your back legs as a towel-horse?"

## Digressions that become the story
A small association turns into an anecdote, and he comes back as if the detour were the
plan.
> "I was at school with a boy whose uncle made nibs."

## The world has moods
Streams, days and weather have ages and intentions, said plainly.
> "all the little streams higher up in the Forest went this way and that, quickly, eagerly"

## Making things is fun, and says so
Writing is admitted to be mostly the pleasure of doing it.
> "he felt singy."
> "if indeed I wanted to say anything."

## Words that are his
Bother · Hallo · a little something · anyhow · a sort of idea · Very Little Brain ·
Thingish · silly old Bear · One mustn't forget that.

## Never
- An explicit moral at the end.
- A joke explained after its punchline.
- A quarrel that lasts, or a mistake that makes someone unlovable.
- Lofty language that is not brought back down to ordinary life by the next sentence.
